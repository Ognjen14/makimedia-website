Getting started

Makimedia turns the videos you already have into a library. It needs to know where they are, and then it does the rest by itself.

On Windows

The first time you open Makimedia, choose Choose a folder and pick the folders where you keep your films and shows. You can pick several at once. Makimedia only reads the folders you pick.

To add or remove folders later, open Settings, Scan folders. Removing a folder takes its files out of the library, but nothing on your disk is ever touched.

On Android phones, tablets and TVs

There are no folders to pick. Tap Allow access to videos, and Makimedia finds the videos on the device by itself, including a memory card. When a new drive is plugged in, it asks whether to scan it.

On a TV you can also choose Continue without them, if your films live on a PC and you only want to stream from it.

The first scan

Makimedia then reads your files and looks up each title on TMDB for its poster, overview and cast. This only takes long the first time. Each title is looked up once and kept on your device afterwards.

No internet? Your videos still appear and play. They're matched when the connection is back.

Naming your files

Makimedia works out what a video is from the name of the file and the folders above it. You don't need to clean names up, but a title and a year go a long way. Films are happiest in a folder of their own:

Films/
  Heat (1995)/
    Heat (1995).mkv

A show wants a folder per show and a folder per season:

Shows/
  The Wire (2002)/
    Season 01/
      The.Wire.S01E01.mkv
  • Put the year in the name. It separates a remake from the original, and a film from a show with the same name.
  • Episodes are recognised from S01E02, 1x02 or Season 1 Episode 2. S00E01 is a special.
  • Everything after the year or episode number is ignored, such as resolution, source, codec or release group.
  • Subtitles are found beside the film when their name starts with the film's, like Heat (1995).en.srt, or in a Subs folder next to it.

The full rules are in the app, under Settings, How your library is read on Windows, phones and tablets.

Streaming from your PC

Your Windows PC can share its whole library with every phone, tablet and TV running Makimedia at home. There's no server to set up and no address to type.

  1. On the PC, open the menu on the side, choose Stream to devices and press Start streaming.
  2. On the phone, tablet or TV, open Makimedia. The PC appears at the top of Home, saying it is streaming. Choose Connect.
  3. Watch. The PC's films and shows appear with their posters and your progress. The video stays on your home network.

You can also connect from Settings, Streaming, which lists every PC streaming on your network. That's also where you disconnect, and this device's own films come back.

The device doesn't find the PC
  1. Set the network to Private. In Windows, open Settings, Network & internet, choose your Wi-Fi or Ethernet connection and set its network profile to Private. On a public network, Windows hides the PC from other devices.
  2. Let Makimedia through the firewall. If Windows asks whether Makimedia may use the network, allow it on private networks. If you dismissed that question, allow Makimedia in Windows Security, Firewall & network protection, Allow an app through firewall.
  3. Check that both are on the same network. A guest Wi-Fi network, or a router that keeps wireless devices apart from each other, stops them from seeing one another.

Makimedia finds the PC by itself, so there's no address to type in. If it still doesn't appear, write to us and choose Streaming help.

Good to know while streaming
  • Nothing plays on the PC while it streams. Its page shows what each device is watching instead. Press Stop streaming to use it again; each device keeps the place it reached.
  • Closing the window can keep it streaming. Turn on Settings, Keep running when the window is closed, and Makimedia waits in the notification area. Right-click its icon to quit.
  • Use the same version everywhere. A device running a different version of Makimedia from the PC is asked to update first.

Questions

Is Makimedia really free?

Yes. No ads, no subscription, no account and no tracking. It's open source under the GNU General Public License, and the code is on GitHub.

Does Makimedia come with films or shows?

No. It's a player, not a streaming service like Netflix. It has no catalogue of its own and plays only files you already have. See the legal notice.

Do I need an internet connection?

Only to fetch posters and details, once per title. Your library and your videos work with the network off, and streaming from your PC only needs your home network.

Do I need a PC?

No. Phones, tablets and TVs play the videos stored on them. A Windows PC is only needed to stream its library to your other devices.

Which devices does it run on?

Windows 10 and 11, Android phones and tablets, and Android TV.

Which file formats does it play?

These videos are added to the library: mkv mp4 avi mov webm m4v ts m2ts mts wmv flv mpg mpeg ogv 3gp divx vob rmvb.

These subtitles are picked up: srt ass ssa sub vtt idx. Whether a particular file plays also depends on what the device's decoder can handle.

Where does my data go?

Your library, watch history and settings stay on your device. To find a poster, Makimedia asks TMDB about the title it's looking up, and nothing else is sent anywhere. Read the privacy policy.

Will Makimedia change or delete my files?

No. It only reads them. Removing a folder or a file from the library leaves it on your disk, and Settings can put anything you removed back.

A film has the wrong poster. How do I fix it?

Open the film or show and use Fix match. Search for the right title and choose it. Your choice is remembered, so a later scan won't undo it, and for a show it fixes every episode at once.

Titles Makimedia wasn't sure about wait under Suggested in the menu, and files it couldn't name at all are under Unmatched.

A video isn't a film or a show. Can I take it out?

Yes. Open it and choose Remove from library from its menu. The file stays on disk, and Settings, Removed from library puts it back.

How do I add subtitles?

Name the subtitle file after the video and keep it beside it, and it loads by itself. While a video plays, you can also choose Add a subtitle file from the subtitles menu.

On Windows, Find subtitles online searches opensubtitles.com. It uses your own free opensubtitles.com account, which you sign in to under Settings, Subtitles.

Where are the keyboard shortcuts and gestures?

In the app, under Settings: Keyboard shortcuts on a PC, Gestures on a touchscreen, and The remote on a TV.

Troubleshooting

A video is missing from the library
  • Check its format is one of the video formats Makimedia reads.
  • On Windows, check that its folder is in Settings, Scan folders, and use Rescan all after adding new files.
  • On Android, Makimedia needs access to all your videos. If only some were allowed, Settings shows a Videos on this device row that fixes it.
  • Maybe it's there without a title. Look under Unmatched in the menu, then use Fix match.
A video won't play

When a file can't be played, Makimedia says so with This one will not play and gives the reason. The most common is that the device's decoder doesn't support the video's format; it may play on another device, such as your PC. Videos in 8K aren't supported.

The app says "File not found"

The file was moved or deleted, or its drive is disconnected. Reconnect the drive, or rescan the folder the file is in now. Until then it stays in your library with its watch history.

Playback stutters

Look at the top of the player while a video plays. Under the title it shows either hw: and the hardware decoder in use, or software decoding. Software decoding of large 4K videos can be too much for smaller devices.

On Windows, Settings, Force hardware decoding also tries decoders that are normally skipped. Turn it off again if you see artefacts or a file stops playing.

Subtitles don't show on Android TV

Picture-based subtitles (PGS, VobSub and DVB, common on Blu-ray and DVD rips) can't be shown on Android TV. The subtitles menu marks them as a picture subtitle the TV can't show. Use a text subtitle such as an srt file instead.

Subtitles are out of sync

While the video plays, open Delay and shift the subtitles against the audio, a tenth of a second at a time.

Streaming doesn't connect or stops

If the device can't see the PC, follow The device doesn't find the PC. If the connection drops while you watch, the film stops where it was and picks up from there the next time you connect.

Write to us, and include your device, what you did and what happened. The log file helps most of all: the Contribute page explains where to find it.
